What exactly was your small-cap shorting strategy? I need to provide some background information first. Companies need cash to stay afloat, and they will raise capital when they need it. For example, let’s say a company has $1 million in working capital and a quarterly burn rate of $500,000. Combining that information, you know the company will need to raise money in the next six months if it wants to stay in business. These companies typically raise capital via secondary offerings,3 and these secondaries tend to be associated with a big gap up in the stock. Typically, secondaries are discounted to the last traded price to incentivize buyers. Why do the small caps gap up? There are only a handful of underwriters in the small-cap space, and they aren’t the big players like JP Morgan or Goldman Sachs. These underwriters acquire the stock (or other financial instruments, such as warrants or convertible bonds) at a low price. They can negotiate very favorable terms because the companies are desperate to raise cash to survive. The underwriters then look to find a way to rally the stock so they can unload their shares at a profit. How do they get the stock to gap up? They issue a press release, which can be recycled old news or completely fabricated. I’ve seen so many fake press releases. Then the stock rallies in pre-market hours, when volume is much lower. Who is buying in the pre-market? It could be the underwriters themselves or retail longs. The stock then gaps sharply higher on the open, allowing the underwriters to sell their shares at a profit.
